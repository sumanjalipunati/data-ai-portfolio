# Turning Manufacturing Data into Clear Operational Insights

**Context:** Data analytics work at CT/BI Solutions  
**Focus:** Manufacturing reporting, data preparation and Power BI

## Where the work started

Manufacturing teams need to understand what is happening across production: how much is being produced, where work is waiting, how much time is spent on rework and how costs compare.

The information needed to answer these questions existed, but preparing it and comparing results across factories involved manual work in Excel. Before teams could investigate performance, they first had to spend time bringing the data together.

My contribution was to help turn that reporting process into a more consistent analytical workflow—from understanding stakeholder needs to preparing data and delivering interactive dashboards.

## Understanding the questions behind the dashboard

I began by gathering and structuring business requirements through stakeholder workshops.

This stage helped define what the reporting needed to cover and which operational questions mattered to the people using it. It also provided the foundation for deciding how data should be organized and which KPIs should appear in the reports.

The goal was to make the information useful for comparing production performance and investigating differences across sites and production stages.

## Preparing the data for analysis

A dashboard depends on the data behind it.

I designed ETL workflows using Power Query and Python to transform raw operational data into clean analytical datasets. This created a repeatable preparation process and reduced the manual work needed before analysis.

Working on this stage reinforced the importance of understanding how source data connects to the business measures shown in a report.

## Building a shared view of performance

Using Power BI and DAX, I developed a data model connecting operational KPIs, including:

- Throughput
- Queue time
- Rework minutes
- Unit cost

The model supported cross-plant comparisons that had previously been performed manually in Excel.

Bringing these measures into a connected model gave the reporting a consistent structure and made it easier to explore performance across factories and production stages.

## Making the results easier to explore

I developed a multi-page Power BI dashboard with dynamic filters and drill-through navigation.

Users could move from an overview into more detailed views, narrowing the report to the factory or production stage relevant to their question.

This made the reporting more useful for investigation. Instead of preparing a separate spreadsheet for each comparison, teams could explore the available views within the dashboard.

## Making reporting repeatable

To support ongoing use, I configured automated daily refresh and structured report distribution in Power BI Service.

This replaced recurring manual reporting steps and helped stakeholder teams access updated information through a more consistent process.

The contribution extended beyond building the initial dashboard: it also addressed how the reporting would be maintained and used over time.

## Why the work mattered

The improvements reduced data preparation and manual reporting effort, giving operations teams more room to focus on interpreting results.

For me, the most valuable part was connecting the technical work to a practical business need. Data preparation, KPI modeling and dashboard design each served the same purpose: helping people understand operational performance more clearly.

## What I learned

This work shaped how I approach analytics:

- Start with the decisions and questions the report needs to support.
- Give data preparation as much attention as visualization.
- Define KPIs clearly so comparisons are meaningful.
- Design reports around how people investigate problems.
- Plan for refresh and distribution so the solution remains useful after delivery.
